Understanding Food Safety & Quality

Food safety is an essential part of food production, processing, packaging, storage and distribution. It ensures that food is safe for consumption and meets required quality standards. Good Hygiene Practices (GHP), Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and Food Quality Control play important roles in maintaining food safety and quality.

01

Prevent Contamination

Follow hygiene practices to reduce biological, chemical and physical contamination.

02

Maintain Standards

Use proper manufacturing procedures and documented processes.

03

Ensure Quality

Inspect and test food products to check whether they meet specifications.

01 / FOOD HYGIENE

Good Hygiene Practices (GHP)

Clean habits and hygienic conditions are the foundation of safe food handling.

What is GHP?

Good Hygiene Practices are basic conditions and activities that help maintain a hygienic environment throughout food handling and preparation. GHP helps prevent contamination and supports the production of safe food.

Key Elements of GHP

  • Personal hygiene and proper handwashing
  • Cleaning and sanitation of equipment
  • Safe water supply
  • Pest control and waste management
  • Prevention of cross-contamination
  • Proper food handling and storage
  • Clean food preparation areas
Practical Example: Food handlers should wash their hands properly, wear clean protective clothing and keep food-contact surfaces clean.
02 / FOOD MANUFACTURING

Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP)

Proper systems and controlled processes help maintain consistent food production.

What is GMP?

Good Manufacturing Practices are guidelines and procedures that help ensure food products are consistently manufactured and controlled according to appropriate quality and safety requirements.

Key Elements of GMP

  • Proper building and facility design
  • Equipment maintenance and calibration
  • Employee training and responsibilities
  • Raw material inspection
  • Process control and monitoring
  • Documentation and record keeping
  • Proper storage and transportation
Practical Example: Food processing equipment should be maintained, cleaned and checked regularly. Production records should be properly documented.
03 / FOOD QUALITY

Food Quality Control

Testing and inspection help maintain product quality, consistency and safety.

What is Food Quality Control?

Food Quality Control (QC) is the process of inspecting, measuring and testing raw materials, in-process products and finished food products to check whether they meet defined quality specifications.

Major Areas of Quality Control

  • Raw material inspection
  • Physical quality testing
  • Chemical analysis
  • Microbiological testing
  • Packaging and labelling inspection
  • Storage condition monitoring
  • Finished product evaluation
Practical Example: A food quality inspector may check colour, odour, texture, pH, moisture, packaging integrity and relevant microbiological parameters.
KNOW THE DIFFERENCE

Food Safety vs Food Quality

Food Safety

Food safety focuses on ensuring that food does not cause harm to consumers when it is prepared and consumed as intended.

Examples:

Pathogen control, allergen management, chemical hazards and foreign matter prevention.

Food Quality

Food quality focuses on whether a product meets expected characteristics and defined specifications.

Examples:

Taste, colour, aroma, texture, consistency, appearance and packaging.
